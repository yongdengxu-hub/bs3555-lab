Why this matters — real examples

Marking to market makes derivatives safe for strangers, by converting default risk into a daily cash-flow discipline that can itself become the crisis.

Monetary policyThe LDI spiral, the very same week

While your simulated account was getting its margin calls, UK pension funds were getting real ones on hedged gilt positions. Their forced gilt sales pushed yields higher, triggering more calls, until the Bank of England stepped in on 28 September 2022 to buy gilts "at whatever scale is necessary". Prudent collateral, demanded by everyone simultaneously, is a fire sale.

→ The link: the top-ups in your margin-call column are what, aggregated across a pension system, forced a central bank intervention.

Financial marketsBeing right eventually is not enough

In the default long-GBP setup, sterling ends the week only about 1% below your entry, yet the account demands roughly $14,000 of fresh cash within two days. Fail Monday's call and you are liquidated at the bottom: right about the recovery, bankrupt anyway. This "ruin risk" is why hedgers with lumpy cash flows often prefer forwards.

→ The link: flip to short GBP and see the same week from the winner's side: the cash arrives daily too.

CareersThe margin desk is a job

Clearing houses (LCH, CME Clearing) employ risk teams whose whole day is setting the initial and maintenance levels you just typed: high enough to cover a bad day's move, low enough not to strangle trading. After 2022 they raised commodity margins sharply and got blamed for both too-late and too-much.

→ The link: re-run the week with initial margin at $1,500 and at $5,000 and you have the desk's dilemma in two clicks.
