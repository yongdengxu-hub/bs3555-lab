In treasury mandate mode you run four monthly rounds. Each round gives you fresh working capital and a new obligation: sometimes you owe a currency, sometimes you will receive one, in dollars or euros. Your job every month is the same, and it is not to make a profit: make the outcome certain. Each round is scored on its own and your term score is the average, so one lucky month cannot carry you.

Prices are real (ECB/FRED). Forwards come from covered interest parity, options from Garman–Kohlhagen with realised volatility. This mirrors the graded Google Sheets version exactly.

Your moneyThe treasurer's job is boring on purpose

Hedge each mandate fully with a forward and your net worth is almost a flat line: 100% hedge effectiveness, near-zero volatility, and you will never top a returns table. That is the correct answer. Unilever's treasury does not try to win at FX; it tries to make next year's costs knowable, which is exactly what Marmitegate was about when the hedges rolled off.

→ The link: press "auto-fill full hedge" every round, then read your volatility column instead of your return column.

Financial marketsA receivable hedges the other way round

Rounds alternate. When you owe dollars you are short dollars, so you buy them forward. When you will receive euros you are long euros, so you sell them forward. Get the direction wrong and you double your risk instead of removing it — the simulator will usually block it for breaching the exposure limit, which is itself the lesson.

→ The link: try hedging a receivable by buying forward and watch what happens to net exposure.

Monetary policyTwo days in September 2022

Start on the mini-budget and take a leveraged futures position at the 5× limit. Over two trading days your equity falls about a quarter, because sterling fell from 1.127 to 1.070 against the dollar. You keep the position only because the leverage cap exists; with the leverage the LDI pension funds were running, this is the week they got margin calls they could not meet.

→ The link: the limit is not bureaucracy. It is why you are still solvent on 27 September.

How to use it in class

Run everyone on the same start date and compare three groups after four rounds: forward hedgers, option hedgers, and the unhedged. Rank them by average hedge effectiveness, not return. The forward group wins on certainty every month; the option group pays a premium to keep upside; the unhedged group often posts the best profit and the worst risk, which is precisely the conversation you want.
