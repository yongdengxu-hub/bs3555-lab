Two ways to play. In treasury mandate you are the treasurer of a UK firm that owes dollars in three months: your job is to make the cost certain, not to make a profit. In speculation you have £1,000 and no obligations. Every price is real: spot from FRED, rates from SONIA / SOFR / EuroSTR, forwards by covered interest parity, options by Garman–Kohlhagen with realised volatility. There is a bid–ask spread on everything.

This is practice mode: history replays instantly, so you get a semester of feedback in ten minutes.

Why this matters — real examples

Every number here is the module's theory working on real prices. Nothing is invented.

Your moneyThe treasurer's job is boring on purpose

In mandate mode a full forward hedge makes your net worth almost a flat line: hedge effectiveness near 100%, volatility near zero, and you will never top the return leaderboard. That is the correct answer. Unilever's treasury does not try to win at FX; it tries to make next year's costs knowable, which is exactly what Marmitegate was about when the hedges rolled off.

→ The link: hedge fully, then look at your volatility column rather than your return column.

Monetary policyTwo days in September 2022

Load the mini-budget scenario, step to 22 September, and go long sterling with futures at the full 5× limit. Over the next two trading days your equity falls about 26%, because sterling fell from 1.127 to 1.070 against the dollar. You keep your position only because the leverage cap is there; with the leverage the LDI pension funds were running, this is the week they got margin calls they could not meet. Realised volatility in the data jumps from 9% to 15%, so every option you buy afterwards is suddenly far dearer.

→ The link: the leverage limit is not bureaucracy. It is the only reason you are still in the game on 27 September.

Financial marketsThe carry trade, discovered by accident

Buy dollars spot and your dollar balance earns SOFR while your pound balance stops earning SONIA. That gap is the carry trade, and it is why the forward sits at a discount: covered interest parity takes the carry back. Hold a spot position for a month and you see both effects in your P&L, separately.

→ The link: run 2022–23 long USD and watch interest, not just the exchange rate, move your NAV.

How to use it in class

Put everyone in mandate mode on the same scenario and compare three groups: forward hedgers, option hedgers, and the unhedged. Then rank them by hedge effectiveness instead of return. The forward group wins on certainty, the option group pays a premium for keeping upside, and the unhedged group learns why treasurers do not gamble with the payables account.
