Two riskless routes take home currency today into foreign currency at time T: swap now and deposit abroad, or deposit at home and sell forward. If both are riskless they must tie, which pins the forward: F = S (1+r)/(1+r*). Drag anything below; the forward follows. Then misquote the forward and watch the arbitrage machine print money, leg by leg.

Why this matters — real examples

CIP is the most-used equation on any FX desk, and its post-2008 failure is one of the biggest puzzles in international finance.

Financial marketsThe basis: the arbitrage nobody closes

Since 2008, borrowing dollars via the FX swap market has cost 20–70bp more per year than borrowing them directly (against JPY), for seventeen years. Banks cannot arbitrage it away because the trade eats balance sheet that Basel III charges for. Du, Tepper and Verdelhan (Journal of Finance, 2018) is the canonical paper.

→ The link: set the deviation slider to −40 pips and imagine a regulator charging you rent on the position: that is the modern FX swap market.

Monetary policyMarch 2020: the Fed as arbitrageur of last resort

In the covid dash-for-dollars the deviation you are simulating blew out to about −140bp; the Fed lent up to $449bn to other central banks through swap lines so their banks could get dollars. The parity was restored by policy, not by textbook arbitrageurs.

→ The link: your "riskless profit" table is exactly the trade the Fed's swap lines replaced.

Your moneyWhy high-rate currencies aren't a free lunch forward

A one-year NZD deposit may pay 3% more than GBP, but the forward rate prices the kiwi at a matching discount: hedged, you earn the GBP rate. Every "guaranteed FX yield pickup" pitch you will ever hear dies on this widget.

→ The link: raise r* and watch F fall by exactly the amount that kills the pickup.

How to use it in class

Set the 2024-25 exam numbers (S = 0.25 scaled, r = 2.7%, r* = 2.6%, quoted forward far off CIP) and let students race to state the arbitrage direction before the legs table reveals it.
