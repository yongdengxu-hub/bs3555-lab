Next period the exchange rate is either S0u or S0d, nothing else. In that tiny world a currency option can be manufactured exactly from a forward plus a deposit, so its price is forced. The exam asks for the calculation by replication and by risk-neutral probability every single year; this page runs all three roads live and shows every intermediate number the mark scheme wants.

The tree

Why this matters — real examples

The two-state trick from 1979 (Cox, Ross and Rubinstein) still prices most of the world's exotic derivatives, one small step at a time.

Financial marketsDesks still use trees, not just formulas

Closed-form prices exist only for plain European options. American features, barriers, and the knock-in/knock-out structures that hurt Korean exporters in 2008 are priced on lattices: exactly your tree, with thousands of steps and the same backward induction.

→ The link: the B and q you just computed are recalculated at every node of those production lattices.

MacroRisk-neutral probabilities are read at central banks

The Bank of England routinely extracts option-implied (risk-adjusted) probability distributions for sterling around events like referendums and budgets. Those published fan-charts of the exchange rate are your q, scaled up from two states to a full distribution.

→ The link: q moved when you dragged r or r*, but never when you changed your private view: that is the whole point.

CareersThe interview question that never dies

"Price a one-period option; now explain why the real probability of up never appeared." Every derivatives graduate scheme asks some version. The three-roads-one-answer structure on this page is the model answer.

How to use it in class

Set the 2025-26 exam's numbers (S0=1.077, u=1.1, d=0.9, r=1%, r*=0.5%, X=1) and check the steps panel against the answer key: C0 = 0.0960. Then break it: push d above F/S0 and watch q leave (0,1), the model's own alarm bell.
