Why this matters — real examples

This four-line calculation built the largest market on earth: $548 trillion of interest-rate swaps outstanding at end-2024 (BIS).

Financial marketsIBM and the World Bank, 1981

IBM held Deutschmark and Swiss franc debt it wanted out of; the World Bank wanted more of exactly that and faced saturation premiums. Salomon Brothers had them service each other's bonds instead of refinancing: the first modern swap, and precisely the comparative-advantage table you just filled in.

→ The link: your "total gain" cell is why the deal happened at all.

Monetary policyThe benchmark had to be replaced

Every floating leg once reset on LIBOR, a survey rate that traders rigged; fines passed $9bn and the rate was executed in 2023. Its successors (SONIA, SOFR, EuroSTR) are computed from actual overnight transactions. When you typed "SONIA + 0.30", you used the reformed plumbing.

→ The link: the floating spreads in your table ride on a benchmark that had to be rebuilt for honesty.

How to use it in class

Type the 2025-26 exam's numbers (A: 3 / SONIA+0.5; B: 4.5 / SONIA+1.0; bank 0.25) and have everyone commit landing rates before revealing: A pays SONIA+0.125, B pays 4.125. Then break comparative advantage (make the spreads equal) and watch the gain vanish.
